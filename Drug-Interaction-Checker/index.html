HOW TO USE RXCLASH

RxClash checks the FDA drug label database for known interactions between medications.

  • Type a drug name in the search box — autocomplete will suggest matches from RxNorm.
  • Press Enter (or click a suggestion) to add it as a chip.
  • Add 2–5 drugs, then click CHECK (or press Enter with an empty field).
  • Results appear on the right; hover nodes/edges on the graph for details.
  • Use the example presets at the bottom for quick demos.

Severity is derived from keywords found in FDA drug interaction text:

Contraindicated — combination must never be used; risk of serious harm or death.
Major — life-threatening or permanent damage possible; avoid unless no alternative.
Moderate — may cause deterioration; monitoring or dose adjustment required.
Minor — limited clinical significance; effects usually manageable.
No data — drug label text found but no interaction keywords matched, or no label retrieved.

Note: severity detection is keyword-based and may not capture all nuances. Always verify with a pharmacist.

A drug interaction occurs when one drug changes the way another drug works in the body. Common mechanisms:

  • Pharmacokinetic — one drug affects how the body absorbs, distributes, metabolizes, or excretes another (e.g., CYP450 inhibition).
  • Pharmacodynamic — two drugs produce additive, synergistic, or antagonistic effects (e.g., two blood thinners increasing bleed risk).
  • Drug-food — not checked here, but grapefruit juice can block metabolism of many drugs.

Over 1.5 million ER visits per year in the US are related to adverse drug events.

RxClash uses two public FDA APIs:

  • RxNorm (NLM) — provides drug name suggestions via approximate term matching.
  • OpenFDA Drug Labels — retrieves the official FDA drug label text, from which the "Drug Interactions" section is extracted and analyzed.

Labels are searched by both generic and brand name. Results are cached per session to reduce API calls.

Limitation: not all drugs have FDA labels in OpenFDA, and FAERS adverse event co-occurrence is not causation.
